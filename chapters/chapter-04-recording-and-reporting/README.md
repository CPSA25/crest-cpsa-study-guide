## About This Chapter

After testing concludes, a penetration tester's most critical responsibility begins: **translating technical findings into actionable business intelligence through professional reporting and meticulous documentation**.

This chapter answers essential questions that CPSA candidates must master:

- **Who** are the key audiences for a penetration test report, and how do their information needs differ?
- **What** evidence and documentation must be captured during an engagement to maintain integrity?
- **When** should different types of reporting occur (interim vs. final)?
- **How** do you structure reports to serve executive management, technical teams, and auditors simultaneously?
- **Why** does Chain of Custody (CoC) matter, and what legal/compliance implications exist in the UK?
- **What** role does CVSS scoring play in translating technical findings into business risk?

**Key Insight:** The penetration test is not complete until the findings are professionally documented, properly communicated, and acted upon. This chapter is the bridge between **testing and remediation**.

## CPSA Exam Preparation

### **Exam Question Patterns You Should Expect:**

✅ **Audience Matching:** "Which report section is intended for the CFO?" / "A developer needs to reproduce a vulnerability—which section should they reference?"

✅ **Severity vs. Risk:** "A vulnerability has a low CVSS score but affects a critical payment system. How should this be reported?"

✅ **Chain of Custody Scenarios:** "Evidence was transferred from Tester A to Tester B. What documentation is mandatory?"

✅ **Report Structure:** "An Executive Summary includes detailed command outputs and code snippets. What's wrong with this approach?"

✅ **UK Compliance:** "A penetration tester discovers PII during testing. Within how many hours must the client notify the ICO?" (Answer: 72 hours per UK GDPR)

✅ **CVSS/Risk Translation:** "How should a High-severity technical vulnerability be presented to non-technical executives?"

### **Study Tips:**

1. **Distinguish Severity from Risk** – This is tested frequently. Understand that CVSS is technical; risk is business-context-dependent.

2. **Master Audience Segmentation** – Know exactly what each audience needs. This skill is tested in multiple question formats.

3. **Understand Chain of Custody Rigorously** – Even small gaps in documentation can compromise evidence. Know all components: timestamps, personnel, transfers, storage, disposal.

4. **Know UK Legal Framework** – CPSA is UK-focused. Understand Computer Misuse Act Section 1 (unauthorized access), DPA 2018 Section 170 (criminal data handling), and the 72-hour breach notification requirement.

5. **Practice Report Structure** – Use the Report Completion Checklist to practice evaluating fictional reports. Ask yourself: "Would this meet exam standards?"

## Tools & Checklists

### **Report Completion Checklist**

A practical, actionable checklist organized into four quality dimensions:

**Executive Summary (5 items)**
- [ ] Two pages maximum
- [ ] No technical jargon
- [ ] Clear risk profile statement
- [ ] 2-3 critical findings summarized
- [ ] Strategic recommendations aligned to business goals

**Technical Report (6 items)**
- [ ] All findings have unique IDs
- [ ] Each finding includes a CVSS score
- [ ] Steps to reproduce are reproducible by the technical team
- [ ] Screenshots/PoC are clear and labeled
- [ ] Remediation addresses root cause (not symptom)
- [ ] Affected systems explicitly listed

**Chain of Custody (5 items)**
- [ ] Personnel signatures logged
- [ ] Transfer timestamps recorded
- [ ] Storage method documented
- [ ] Sensitive data encrypted
- [ ] Destruction procedures detailed

**UK Compliance (5 items)**
- [ ] Rules of Engagement referenced (Computer Misuse Act defence)
- [ ] Scope and limitations stated
- [ ] Testing methodology documented (OWASP/PTES)
- [ ] PII handling procedures described
- [ ] 72-hour breach notification procedure clarified

## Key Concepts & Definitions

### **Chain of Custody (CoC)**
The meticulous, chronological documentation process that tracks every interaction with digital evidence from collection through final disposition. Ensures integrity, prevents tampering, and supports legal defensibility.

### **Executive Summary**
A one-page, non-technical briefing for senior management presenting overall risk ranking, business impact, and strategic recommendations in plain language.

### **Technical Report**
A detailed, technical document for developers and engineers containing vulnerability descriptions, PoC steps, evidence, and specific remediation instructions.

### **CVSS (Common Vulnerability Scoring System)**
An industry-standard framework (0.0–10.0 scale) providing objective, reproducible measurement of vulnerability severity based on exploitability and impact characteristics.

### **Proof of Concept (PoC)**
Step-by-step demonstration, including screenshots, command outputs, and code snippets, proving that a vulnerability can be successfully exploited.

### **Risk Ranking (vs. Severity)**
Business-focused assessment translating technical severity into organizational risk based on data sensitivity, criticality, compliance implications, and business context.

### **UK GDPR 72-Hour Rule**
Requirement that organizations notify the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO) of personal data breaches within 72 hours of discovery, unless the breach is unlikely to create risk.
